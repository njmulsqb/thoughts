Back in October 2020, I watched *The Social Dilemma*—a Netflix documentary—on the recommendation of a friend. It was a documentary about the side effects of social media, and those side effects were mentioned by none other than the manufacturers of those tech giants. People holding executive and engineering positions in Facebook, Google, YouTube, Pinterest, and elsewhere explained how social media is becoming a social dilemma of this century. I won't go into detail about that documentary, but it ignited a spark within me.

> In response to *The Social Dilemma*, I've made lots of changes to my internet routine, one of them is shifting to [DuckDuckGo](https://twitter.com/DuckDuckGo) with [Firefox](https://twitter.com/firefox). It's way better than Google if you consider privacy. Must give it a try!
>
> — Najam Ul Saqib, [October 12, 2020](https://twitter.com/NjmUlSqb/status/1315553239522332672)

I started questioning the role of social media in our lives. I won't say social media is all bad, but the way it has been designed, most of us get hooked to it without even knowing it. I started discussing it with my friends, and one of them suggested I watch the *Snowden* movie, as it reveals much more about the evil faces of these tech giants and government surveillance.

I would highly recommend you watch this movie. *Snowden* is the story of a hero named Edward Snowden, who worked in the NSA (National Security Agency). He saw there that the government was doing wrong by invading the privacy of all users. They had encryption keys from tech giants and could access and see emails, texts, photos, and any other sensitive media. His story is very interesting and you should give it some time if you're serious about your privacy.

The government could use the mic and camera of your phone and laptop and see you anytime without your consent. It was horrible. Most of our conversations are online now, and because every conversation you make, every photo you share, and every call you make is being listened to and observed carefully, it is really not acceptable for any of us.

After searching more and more on this, watching movies, reading articles, and listening to podcasts, I decided to bring a change in my online life.

I started to bring drastic changes, like avoiding Google as much as I could. I shifted to [DuckDuckGo](https://duckduckgo.com) as my primary search engine. It felt awkward for a couple of days, but then it was just like I was using Google. DuckDuckGo produced great search results and stored none of my information or searches. I dived deeper into the privacy settings of my Google account and disabled many privacy-invading settings that had been unknown to me previously.

![A laptop and notebook on a desk](https://1.bp.blogspot.com/-NmheyL7pq_s/YBpUrlXxXiI/AAAAAAAAgOw/KdyM8eUIoRYkO9yN7YcLtBUxFwc3uczLACLcBGAsYHQ/s2048/dawit-FBmEunYW1Yg-unsplash.jpg)

I left Google Photos and backed up my sensitive data from Google Drive as well.

Now come to the browser. I was using Google Chrome as my main browser, but after all this, I had to say goodbye to it as well. I installed [Mozilla Firefox](https://firefox.com) on all of my operating systems, including desktop and mobile. [Brave](https://brave.com) is my secondary browser. Both of these browsers are privacy-focused and don't track you around or store your personal information.

I made the transition towards encrypted email providers. The best of them was [ProtonMail](https://protonmail.com), and I use ProtonMail as my professional email instead of Gmail. I tried [Tutanota](https://tutanota.com) as well, but I think they needed to work a lot on their service yet. ProtonMail is a mature and feature-rich service.

I backed up most of my private data into physical external drives and encrypted them, so there's no chance left for data leakage from there. If and only if I still need to use cloud storage for private data, I use [Mega](https://mega.nz) for it. Mega stores your data in encrypted form and they can't check your data, so this is my cloud storage these days. Though ProtonMail is also planning to introduce their cloud service in the future, they're trying to provide an alternative to Google by providing services like mail, calendar, VPN, and so on. I might consider buying their subscription in the future as well. I use extensions like DuckDuckGo Privacy Essentials, Privacy Badger, and uBlock that help me protect myself from trackers spread all over the internet.

The most challenging part was leaving WhatsApp because all of my contacts used WhatsApp, so I was going to face difficulties after deleting it. But to be honest, it was just fear and nothing else. Obviously, I was doing this months before the notorious WhatsApp policy got revealed and everyone started to migrate to Signal and Telegram. That policy was just a highlight of how your data is being used and sold by corporations and intelligence agencies. As soon as I deleted WhatsApp, people who wanted to contact me found other alternatives and there was no hindrance in communication.

For chat and messaging, I completely avoid text messages and use [Signal](https://signal.org) and [Telegram](https://telegram.org) now for personal messaging. Both of these apps are amazing. My personal favourite is Signal: it's open source and supported through donations. I use it daily and it is also used by many privacy-conscious people, including Edward Snowden.

As a matter of fact, I have also put tape on my laptop's camera 😂. You might think it too extremist, but if you take a deep dive into the world of mass surveillance and data privacy, you'll come to know how exposed we are and how important these steps are.

I am much more confident about my online presence now. I don't use Facebook or any of its products like WhatsApp or Instagram. My online presence is much more secure and private now and hopefully, after all these steps, some of my data will be secure, if not all.
